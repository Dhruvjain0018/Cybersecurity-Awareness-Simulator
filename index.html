Safety Tips

Pick a topic to learn quick, practical advice.

Hover before you click — always check the full URL first.
  • Check the full URL, not just the first part
  • Avoid links in unexpected emails or texts
  • Type addresses directly into your browser when in doubt
Strong passwords protect your accounts.
  • Use unique passwords for each account
  • Use a password manager
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)
Emails are the most common attack method.
  • Check sender email carefully
  • Avoid clicking unknown attachments
  • Do not share personal information via email
Public Wi-Fi can be unsafe.
  • Avoid banking on public Wi-Fi
  • Use VPN for security
  • Disconnect after use

FAQ (Cybersecurity Awareness)

Phishing is a cyber attack where attackers pretend to be a trusted source (like a bank or company) to trick users into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, OTPs, or credit card details. It often happens through fake emails, messages, or websites that look real.
You can identify a phishing website by checking the URL carefully. Look for spelling mistakes, extra words, or suspicious domains. Also check for HTTPS (secure connection), poor design quality, and unexpected login requests.
Immediately change your password for that account and any other accounts using the same password. Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA), run a virus scan, and inform the concerned service provider. If financial details were shared, contact your bank immediately.
Two-Factor Authentication adds an extra layer of security by requiring a second verification step, such as an OTP sent to your phone or email, in addition to your password. Even if someone steals your password, they cannot access your account without the second factor.
Strong passwords prevent attackers from easily guessing or cracking your account. A strong password should be long, unique, and include a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. Avoid using the same password across multiple websites.
Scam emails often contain urgent language ("Act now!"), suspicious links, unknown sender addresses, spelling mistakes, and attachments. They may try to create panic or excitement to trick you into clicking links.
